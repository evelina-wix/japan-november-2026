# Japan, slowly and well

## A personal travel proposal · 1–18 November 2026

Prepared 19 September 2026. Two adults · regional culture · onsen · good food · ryokans.

My overall recommendation is **Route A**, revised to seven stays. **Route B** is the slowest and **Route C** the strongest food/onsen/event alternative. **Route D** is an optional eastern circuit if its individual experiences particularly appeal. For longer Tokyo and Kyoto stays, **E is my preferred balance**, **F the fewest moves**, and **G keeps the mountain towns**. Instagram supplies ideas; it does not determine the ranking. Routes are judged on November timing, culture, food, bathing, budget and practical travel.

## Broader route research: how the shortlist was chosen

I compared established nationwide itineraries and regional tourism guidance, then adapted them for a repeat visitor arriving at Haneda on November 1. Rankings below are my planning judgement, not an official best-route award. Instagram is used for optional place and hotel ideas.

### Central + western Honshu — Best overall fit — revised Route A

Tokyo → Matsumoto/Narai → Takayama → Kanazawa → Kinosaki → Hiroshima/Miyajima → Tokyo

Combines historic towns, crafts, seafood, a dedicated onsen town and a varied island/city finish. JNTO and Japan-guide both use the Takayama–Kanazawa cultural corridor in wider Japan itineraries; this adaptation removes places you have already visited and reduces Tokyo/Kyoto emphasis.

**Trade-off:** Two longer travel days around Kinosaki and a long return from Hiroshima. Keep mountain outings optional. The revised plan removes Kurashiki as an overnight base, adding time to Kanazawa and Hiroshima.

[Route/region reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/gc/itineraries/long-plan2/) · [Supporting reference](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e2400_best.html)

### Central Honshu at a slower pace — Best for fewer moves — Route B

Tokyo → Matsumoto/Narai → Takayama → Kanazawa → Yamanaka Onsen → Tokyo

Concentrates the same cultural strengths into a smaller area and places the onsen stay close to Kanazawa. Best if settling into a few places matters more than seeing many regions.

**Trade-off:** Less geographic variety; four Takayama nights and three ryokan nights suit a deliberately slow traveller. Move a night to Kanazawa if you want more urban choice.

[Route/region reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/itineraries/) · [Supporting reference](https://www.yamanaka-spa.or.jp/global/eng/view2.html)

### Northern Kyushu loop — Best for food, onsens and events — Route C

Fukuoka → Nagasaki → Kumamoto → Kurokawa → Beppu/Yufuin → Fukuoka, with Tokyo airport nights

A strong independent recommendation even without the saved reels. JNTO identifies October–November as a traditional autumn window in Kurokawa/Kuju, and the confirmed November sumo dates give the route a specific 2026 anchor.

**Trade-off:** Adds two domestic flights if Seoul departure is from Tokyo. Mountain buses need reservations and dated timetable checks. Aso scenery is optional and volcanic access is conditional.

[Route/region reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/1957/) · [Supporting reference](https://sumo.pia.jp/en/)

### Eastern onsen and Fuji circuit — Optional preference-led alternative — Route D

Tokyo → Minakami → Karuizawa → Kawaguchiko → Ito → Shuzenji → Tokyo

Good when riverside baths, Fuji and the Izu coast are personal must-sees. The saved posts provide useful individual hotels and experiences here; their presence in the collection does not establish this as the best overall route.

**Trade-off:** Several sideways transfers despite staying near Tokyo. Karuizawa may be past peak colour, while Shuzenji may peak after your departure. Six nights in Izu make sense only if slow bathing/coastal days appeal.

[Route/region reference](https://visitkaruizawa.com/en/things/seasons/fall/) · [Supporting reference](https://www.shuzenji-kankou.com/autumn.html)

### Setouchi + Shikoku — Strong additional contender for a rural, active trip

Possible replacement framework: Onomichi/Setoda → Matsuyama/Dogo → Kochi → Iya/Oboke → Takamatsu, with optional Naoshima

JNTO publishes a substantial Shikoku itinerary joining island cycling, valleys, heritage and art. This would introduce a genuinely different region: Dogo bathing, Iya scenery, Kochi food and Setouchi islands.

**Trade-off:** Not yet a costed fifth package. Rural connections, cycling appetite and whether you want a rental car or driver matter more here. JNTO notes Iya's remote setting and slow mountain roads. Worth developing if these experiences outrank the central historic towns.

[Route/region reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/au/itineraries/shikoku-adventures/) · [Supporting reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/spot/ma_188/)

### Southern Tohoku — Credible alternative, rather than a far-northern autumn chase

Possible replacement framework: Sendai/Matsushima → Naruko → Yamagata/Yamadera → Aizu-Wakamatsu, then Tokyo

Regional food, quieter cultural cities and hot springs suit your interests. Lower valleys can retain colour later than the high mountains; the first part of November is the sensible place for this module.

**Trade-off:** Do not extrapolate one foliage window to all Tohoku. The high Hakkoda season is earlier, and Hachimantai's road typically closes from early November. This framework needs its own hotel and transport checks before booking.

[Route/region reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/uk/inspiration/tohoku-travel-guide-uncovering-japans-best-kept-secret/) · [Supporting reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/destinations/tohoku/iwate/hachimantai-area/)

### Standard Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka circuit — Useful benchmark, weaker match for this particular trip

Common first-trip backbone: Tokyo → Kyoto/Osaka with regional excursions

Useful for understanding the major rail corridor and classic highlights, but a generic best-of itinerary is not automatically the best fit for a repeat visitor.

**Trade-off:** Osaka, Kamakura and Nara stay excluded. Kyoto can remain an optional two-night substitution if you want it; it should not dominate a trip expressly focused on other cities. Early-to-mid November is not a guarantee of peak Kyoto colour.

[Route/region reference](https://www.japan-guide.com/e/e2400_tokyo_12.html) · [Supporting reference](https://www.japan.travel/en/guide/november/)

### Mix and match

- Shorter travel: replace Kinosaki with Yamanaka Onsen after Kanazawa, then continue to Hiroshima. You lose the canal bath-town experience but reduce the remote rail detour.

- Extra heritage or coast: take one night each from Kanazawa and Hiroshima and add two in Kurashiki or Onomichi; this returns the plan to eight stays and needs re-timing. It is optional, not the default.

- One island night: use Miyajima instead of Hiroshima on 15 Nov. Forward luggage or travel light; this adds a hotel change.

- No Kyoto sightseeing is needed: Kyoto is only the practical railway interchange to Kinosaki.

## When nature is best

| 13–18 Nov | Yamanaka gorge; Miyajima; Shuzenji in D | Better odds of colour than at the start, but Miyajima can peak later. Tokyo and Kyoto may still be early. |

## The onsen town shortlist

**Kinosaki**: canals, yukata and public bath hopping; best fit in western Honshu. **Kurokawa**: forest and river baths, quiet inns; best fit in Kyushu. **Beppu**: a real city with everyday bathing and steam cooking. **Yamanaka**: gorge and crafts, easy after Kanazawa. **Shibu**: nine small guest-access baths near Nagano. **Ginzan**: gas-lit riverside inns, but a long detour and no guaranteed November snow.

For this trip, choose one main onsen region and enjoy the hotel baths elsewhere.

### Matsumoto + Narai · 2–3 nights

Castle town, craft shops and an exceptionally preserved post town, with a manageable first move from Tokyo.

**November:** Put this near the beginning; urban walks remain worthwhile even if high mountains are past colour.

**Eat:** Shinshu soba, oyaki dumplings, apples and local sake.

**Trade-off:** Narai is in Nagano, a different place from Nara. Do not add a long mountain hike to the jet-lag day.

### Takayama + Hida-Furukawa · 3–4 nights

- Day 3: Hida-Furukawa canals and carpentry heritage, or Hida Folk Village for vernacular houses.

- Extra slow day: cafés, craft shopping and a second neighbourhood walk.

**Trade-off:** The Takayama Autumn Festival is October 9–10, outside your dates. Mountain excursions are optional, not the reason to commit to this base.

- Extra day: a craft-focused outing or an entirely unscheduled ryokan day.

**Trade-off:** Not the same bath-hopping experience as Kinosaki. Do not assume riverside café seating is operating in November; the older official page gives an April–October season.

### Nagasaki · 3 nights

[Google Maps search](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Nagasaki+Japan) · [Booking.com area search](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Nagasaki+Japan&group_adults=2&no_rooms=1&group_children=0)

One of the most distinctive cities here: port history, Chinese and European influences, hills and generous food culture.

**November:** A cultural choice; lower-city foliage is not its main appeal in early November.

**Eat:** Champon, sara udon, castella and a shippoku meal if you want a special dinner.

### Hakone — onsen between the cities · 2–3 nights

[Google Maps search](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=Hakone+%E2%80%94+onsen+between+the+cities+Japan) · [Booking.com area search](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.html?ss=Hakone+%E2%80%94+onsen+between+the+cities+Japan&group_adults=2&no_rooms=1&group_children=0)

The simplest onsen module between Tokyo and Kyoto, with art, woodland and lake scenery alongside bathing.

**November:** Mountain foliage varies by elevation; weather can disrupt the ropeway or hide Fuji. Baths and museums make a useful fallback.

**Eat:** Ryokan dinners, soba and local sweets.

- Arrival: reach the inn by mid-afternoon and enjoy dinner and baths.

- Full day 1: Open-Air Museum and Gora area; retain time at the inn.

- Full day 2: Lake Ashi/ropeway if conditions suit, or a slow bathing day. Hakone Yuryo offers reservable private day baths.

**Trade-off:** Hakone is a spread-out region, not a compact bath-hopping town like Kinosaki. Use Odawara for the Kyoto train; allow for the local leg and a suitable shinkansen connection.

References: [hakone](https://hakone-japan.com/plan-your-trip/access/), [yuryo](https://www.hakoneyuryo.jp/english/).

## Practical decisions

**Shopping change on your arrival date.** Japan changes tax-free purchases to a refund method from 1 November 2026. Older saved shopping reels may describe the previous system. Check the official Japan Tourism Agency instructions linked below.

**Book the special nights first.** Check Kinosaki or Kurokawa half-board rooms, then the remaining hotels. For sumo, official sales have started on the research date; check seats before committing to the Kyushu route.

**Rail passes.** Do not buy a national JR Pass automatically. The official online ordinary prices are ¥50,000 / ¥80,000 / ¥100,000 for 7 / 14 / 21 days; overseas-agent prices change from 1 October 2026. Compare the final paid legs and coverage. The central route includes non-JR buses; Kyushu buses and domestic flights are not covered by a JR rail pass.

**Regional passes.** For Route C, Northern Kyushu rail passes are listed at ¥15,000 for 3 days and ¥17,000 for 5 days, with online pricing potentially different. A pass needs concentrated rail days; the slow loop is not an automatic saving. For Route A, price the western segment against JR West products after dates are chosen.

**Reservations and transfers.** Kanazawa–Kyoto requires a change at Tsuruga. Reserve the onward limited express to Kinosaki. Reserve mountain and Shirakawa-go buses; a village stop requires two bus bookings. Transfer times in this guide are approximate, not confirmed November departures.

**Luggage.** Forward one main suitcase between longer city stays and carry a small bag for the ryokan segment. Allow delivery lead time rather than assuming next-day delivery. This is particularly useful for Shirakawa-go and mountain buses.

**Ryokan timing.** Plan to arrive about 15:00–16:00 and keep dinner time free. Confirm dietary requests before choosing a nonrefundable meal plan. Many ryokans quote per person; always price the final booking for two.

**Bathing together.** Choose a reservable private bath (kashikiri) or the exact room with a private onsen. An ensuite bathtub or an open-air bath does not automatically contain natural hot-spring water. Kinosaki public bathhouses allow tattoos, but individual inns can set different policies.

**Weather and daylight.** Plan outdoor walks for the morning and early afternoon; expect dusk around 17:00 in much of the route. Pack layers and rain protection; mountain valleys can be much colder than the cities.

**Airport sequence.** The reconstructed Riga connection is 40 minutes and Shanghai connection 2h15. These are arithmetic from likely flights, not a validation of your tickets or transfer eligibility. Confirm ticketing, baggage and terminals once you supply the booking.

**Late Haneda arrival.** Book a hotel that accepts late arrival. An airport-area stay is simplest for the Kyushu route; a Tokyo city base can be more convenient for the Matsumoto departure next morning. Check the actual final train or shuttle after your flight is confirmed.
